# Brestanica

Brestanica is a settlement in Slovenia near Krško, historically known as Rajhenburg. It lies at the confluence of the Sava River and the Brestanica stream. The settlement is dominated by Rajhenburg Castle and was first mentioned in 895. Just before the Sava River breaks free from the Posavje hills and spreads into the Krško plain, the Brestanica stream flows into it. The former town of Rajhenburg was squeezed into a gorge between the castle hill and the neighboring wine-growing Sremič. After the war, it was renamed after the stream to today's Brestanica, which retreated northwards and expanded into an idyllic basin reaching to the foot of Bohor. The green slopes with scattered small churches and villages are a treasure trove of natural beauty, inviting excursions.

The castle offers stories of feudal lords, especially the well-known Lords of Rajhenburg; reveals the secret life of Trappist monks who managed it for almost 60 years; and stands as a silent witness to the violence of the occupier and the suffering of people who had to leave it on a path to exile. Exhibits in the museum speak of all this.

In the basin, the Basilica of Mary of Lourdes invites visitors; in the embrace of greenery, well-kept ponds offer peace; and from Mohor's church on Sremič, a unique panorama of Brestanica and its near and distant surroundings unfolds.

The beauty of the landscape, friendly people, good culinary offerings, and wonderful hiking trails in harmony with the interesting past of the place and the castle offer visitors enough opportunities to spend an unforgettable day in Brestanica.

Brestanica is an urban settlement of town character and is the seat of the local community (krajevna skupnost). The area of the Brestanica local community is 1.9 km², the elevation is 177.9 m, and the population is 973 (2002 census). To a casual traveler, Brestanica does not immediately make a strong impression, as the road into the place runs through a narrow valley, which at the end of the old town center slowly widens into a pleasant view of the whole. The settlement contains the Adam Bohorič Brestanica primary school, the Palček kindergarten, a petrol station, numerous shops, inns, a sports and recreation center with a swimming pool, the Brestanica Thermal Power Plant (Termoelektrarna Brestanica), Duropack, and several craft businesses.

Brestanica is among the oldest places in Slovenia and consists of an urban settlement of town character and an older part, which was originally a village settlement below the church of St. Peter and Paul above the meander of the Brestanica stream. The younger market settlement dates from the second half of the 14th century. It developed on both sides of the road and formed a small rectangular square in the middle. The third, upper part of the square developed only in the 18th century between the church of St. Sebastian (sv. Boštjan) and the mansion on Gorica (above today's Thermal Power Plant). The market settlement also included the riverside part from the railway station, past the lower castle to the Sevnica road.

Due to the crossroads of roads to Sevnica, Brežice, and Podsreda, and the importance of the ford, a new settlement emerged north of the original village settlement in the 14th century, which became a market town. Rajhenburg is said to have received market rights in 1322, but the first written mention of the market dates to 1432. Because it was unfortified, it was exposed to invasions by Turks and Hungarians. In 1668, market and judicial rights were reorganized. The market received judicial authority, the power to issue decrees, and to accept appeals. The authority was granted under the title "Protocol of the Princely Market of Lower Rajhenburg in Styria." The market judge had to take an oath: "I, the market judge of Lower Rajhenburg, promise and swear that I will help the esteemed municipal council and together the entire community, especially the poor, as well as the rich and the wealthy, and the destitute, to assert their freedoms. I guarantee this with my property. I will act and support everything correctly and will not force anyone nor allow anyone to be accused, and I myself will do nothing, nor force anyone into improper dealings. I will do what has always been proper for a market judge. Let everything be safe, without violence and hatred, but in the spirit of friendship. So help me God and the Holy Gospel."

In the period between 1776 and 1799 (the years inscribed on the market pranger), the market experienced several important changes to its status; most notably, it became a market municipality and the seat of a district.

The events of 1848 shook almost all of Europe. When Emperor Ferdinand I the Good approved a constitution and freedom of the press in March, the market of Rajhenburg also rejoiced. Soon after, disagreements began to appear, as the peasants had a mistaken opinion about the "freedom" from obligations to the lordship. While the peasants opposed order, the townspeople gathered in the square. In 1851, Emperor Franz Joseph I granted the market of Rajhenburg the right to hold four annual livestock fairs (1st - Maundy Thursday, 2nd - Monday before Pentecost, 3rd - Monday after Corpus Christi, 4th - the day after St. Peter's Day).

Life in the market was lively and commercially very diverse, as numerous merchants and craftsmen lived and worked there. In 1820, there were as many as 32 (a tailor, 2 shoemakers, 3 potters, 3 blacksmiths, 3 weavers, 5 tanners, 1 gunsmith, 3 merchants, 3 masons, 2 carpenters, a cooper, a butcher, 3 millers, and a joiner). Written sources state that the market houses were ground-floor, wooden and brick, with outbuildings that were mostly wooden. It should not be forgotten that from 1774, a public trivial school operated in the market, hosted in various houses. A parish school had existed since 1681.

Numerous natural disasters also affected the market residents (floods, fires, minor earthquakes, cholera in 1820). Almost every year there were exceptional downpours, as the Sava was said to attract storms. "On St. Ulrich's Day (4 July) 1742 in the afternoon, there was the worst storm. The sky darkened greatly, a gale arose that carried people and demolished buildings, roofs flew through the air. When the gale subsided and it became lighter, a huge hailstorm poured down, which mortally wounded people and killed livestock at pasture. The people suspected that the storm was caused by the parish priest, who boasted that he knew the black art, and who was not at home that day. The next day, people attacked and devastated the vicarage; the priest saved himself by fleeing. Four ringleaders of the uprising: Lovrenc Kink, mayor Sotošek, the Podsreda subject Sikovšek, and the Rajhenburg subject Ferlin were tried – Kink and Sikovšek were beheaded, the other two sentenced to fortress imprisonment. In memory of this event, a pillar was erected at the end of the market along the road to Sevnica, into which the heads of the executed were placed; later they were buried under the pillar. The pillar was removed when the road was widened." (summarized from Brestanica, a collection of articles and discussions, 1982)

The arrival of the Trappists at Rajhenburg Castle in 1881 also meant a revival of the market's pulse. The presence of the Trappists was full of peace and quiet (they adhered to the principle PRAY AND WORK). They inspired the locals with the renovation of the castle, the estate, and modernized farming.

In 1941, the course of development was interrupted. Life changed, as the German regime took over (German schools, compulsory German language…). After 1950, the inhabitants slowly began to rebuild, but the current politics and a more modern way of life drove the inhabitants even further from the market. The craft pulse of 1820 never returned.

Today, the market comes to life once a year, as the Brestanica Tourist Association, in cooperation with other associations in the place, organizes the St. Peter's Fair event (last Saturday in June). With the event, they try to revive the customs, habits, and obligations of the inhabitants of this once so important market. For some, that time is only a faint memory, for most, a complete unknown.

A walk along the street of the Rajhenburg market awakens curiosity in a history lover…, a desire… and at the same time sadness, when the curious eye stops at buildings being gnawed by the tooth of time.

The pillory, pranger, is an approximately 1.5 m high stone, cylindrical in shape, which stood in the middle of the square. On Sundays, quarrelsome men and women, brawlers, night revelers, as well as innkeepers, butchers, and bakers who cheated on measures and weights were tied to it. Passers-by mocked and spat at them.

## History

Nedaleč od Krškega, v smeri proti Celju, leži kraj Brestanica, nekoč imenovan Rajhenburg.

Kraj ima bogato zgodovinsko preteklost. Prve arheološke najdbe na tem področju naj bi bile že iz starejše železne dobe, iz časa od 1000 – 400 let pred našim štetjem. V času Rimskega imperija je mimo Brestanice vodila rimska cesta Neviodunum-Celea. Ostanki treh miljnikov in starega kamnitega mostu nasproti železniške postaje Brestanica pričajo, da naj bi prav tu cesta prečkala reko Savo.

V srednjem veku se kraj prvič omenja leta 838, ko naj bi k mejnemu grofu Savinjske marke Salachonu pribežal slovansko-panonski knez Pribina. Sicer pa se kraj omenja v pisnem viru iz 29. septembra 895, ko je bila v Öttingu na Bavarskem izdana darovnica vzhodno-frankovskega kralja Arnulfa, s katero je bavarskemu velikašu Valtunu podelil tri kraljeve kmetije, tik Save pri Rajhenburgu in eno pri Krškem, v trajno last. Valtunova posest je bila zaradi vdora Madžarov v začetku 10. stoletja izgubljena, v drugi polovici 10. stoletja pa je nemški cesar nekdanjo Valtunovo posest podaril savinjskemu krajišniku Viljemu. Po njegovi smrti je njegova žena grofica Hema Pilštanjska odšla v samostan in posest podarila salzburški nadškofiji. Salzburški nadškof Konrad je v Rajhenburgu na novo pozidal grajsko stavbo in tja poslal ministerialno rodbino, ki je povzela ime po gradu in se poimenovala rodbina Rajhenburških. Imeli so nalogo, da skrbijo za obrambo salzburške posesti v Posavju. Salzburški nadškofje so se trudili, da bi to področje naselili s kmečkim prebivalstvom v čim večjem številu. Zaradi dolgotrajnih vpadov Ogrov je tu živelo malo prebivalcev, pokrajina pa je bila opustošena. Gospodarsko-upravno središče Rajhenburga z okoliškimi vasmi je bilo v Brežicah. Ofical, vodja urada, je moral skrbeti, da so bile kmetije naseljene, da je bila zemlja obdelana in da so tlačani ob pravem času in redno oddajali dajatve in opravljali predpisane služnosti. O tem govore zapisi v urbarju iz leta 1309.

Število vasi v oklici Rajhenburga ni bilo dosti manjše kot je danes, manjše pa je bilo število prebivalcev. Na čelu vasi je bil župan, ki je bil predstavnik zemljiškega gospoda. Število naseljenih kmetij se ni povečalo niti v 14. niti 15. stoletju, kar govore zapisi v urbarjih. Temu so bile krive naravne katastrofe in proti koncu 15. stoletja vpadi Turkov.

V času slovenskega kmečkega upora 1515 so Rajhenburški imeli poleg svoje zemljiške posesti še gospostva Sevnico, Brežice, Kozje in Leskovec pri Krškem. Okrog 9000 upornih kmetov je požgalo brežiški grad in mesto. Polastili so se tudi rajhenburškega gradu. A uporni kmetje so bili kmalu poraženi in kaznovani za svoja dejanja. V slovensko-hrvaškem kmečkem uporu 1573 so se zemljiški gospodje odkupili upornim kmetom tako, da so jim dali hrano.

Kraj Rajhenburg je dobil leta 1432 tržne pravice. Te so jim bile ponovno podeljene 24. septembra 1668 in začeli so pisati zapisnike o sodnih zaslišanjih, odlokih, tožbah in drugo. 1497 je dobil trg od cesarja Maksimiljana pravico do visokega sodstva, ki je bila 1568 ponovno potrjena. 1850 so bile sodne pravice rajhenburškim gospodom odvzete in ustanovljeno je bilo okrajno glavarstvo v Brežicah. 1851 je cesar Franc Jožef I. trgu Rajhenburg podelil pravico do štirih sejmov.

Skozi stoletja so bili grajski gospodje tudi lastniki rajhenburškega zemljiškega gospostva.

Leta 1861 je bila do Rajhenburga zgrajena železnica in leto pozneje je peljal prvi vlak iz Zagreba proti Zidanem Mostu. 1863 pa je Rajhenburg dobil pošto. Kraj se je iz Rajhenburga preimenoval v Brestanico 19. aprila 1952

## Events

- **Pohod po poti trapistov (May 2026)**: On Saturday, 9 May 2026, Turistično društvo Brestanica organized the traditional "Pohod po poti trapistov" (Hike along the Trappist Path). The annual hike commemorates the Trappist monks, known for their diligent cultivation of surrounding estates and for introducing many innovations and knowledge to the area. They were the first in Slovenia to start producing chocolate.
- **Brestanica Open 2025**: On Saturday, 5 July 2025, the traditional tournament BRESTANICA OPEN 2025 took place on the sand volleyball courts. It was organized by former club players Jerneja Planinc and Špela Zakšek in cooperation with Odbojkarsko društvo Brestanica. The tournament was attended by players from all over Slovenia and was the last event among many.
- **Kolesarjenje okoli Brestanice (June 2025)**: On Saturday, 21 June 2025, Turistično društvo Brestanica organized the traditional 14th cycling tour around Brestanica in celebration of the holiday of Krajevna skupnost Brestanica. A diverse cycling group gathered in the square of Brestanica. For the youngest participants and their companions, a flat cycling route was chosen, leading along the road to Špar in Senovo, followed by a descent along the cycling path.
- **Franc Resnik's 100th Birthday**: On 4 April 1925, Franc Resnik was born as the youngest son in the large Resnik family of 11 children in Lokev pri Brestanici. In 2025, he celebrated his 100th birthday, becoming the first male centenarian in Krajevna skupnost Brestanica.
- **Nočni pohod na grad Rajhenburg (December 2024)**: On Thursday, 26 December 2024, Turistično društvo Brestanica organized the traditional night hike to Rajhenburg Castle. Participants gathered in front of the Mercator store in Brestanica. After a short greeting and route description, the hike began across the Senovski potok bridge. The long column of hikers then ascended through Šite past orchards, to the former Trappist factories of chocolate and liqueurs.

## Attractions

**Rajhenburg Castle** stands on a steep rocky promontory above the Sava River. It is among the oldest castles in Slovenia. It offers visitors numerous permanent exhibition collections: Trappists in Rajhenburg, Slovene Exiles 1941-1945, Decorations – Primož Kozmus and others, as well as temporary exhibitions, events, and a summer cinema.

**Basilica of Mary of Lourdes** is among the largest churches in Slovenia and certainly the largest in Posavje. It was built between 1908 and 1914. It is a mighty three-nave church in neo-Romanesque style and ranks among the most beautiful historicist sacral buildings in Slovenia. The church is one of seven basilicas in Slovenia and has received the title Slovenski Lurd (Slovenian Lourdes).

**Brestanica Square** – a walk along the street of the Rajhenburg market awakens curiosity in a history lover, a desire, and at the same time sadness when the curious eye stops at buildings being gnawed by the tooth of time.

**Brestanica Ponds** – a pleasant environment, a fishing challenge, excellent food, and hospitality repeatedly attract people to the ponds in Mačkovci near Brestanica. Fishing enthusiasts can catch carp, grass carp, rudd, tench, crucian carp, and other fish species. Food lovers can enjoy fish and other tasty dishes and wines from the Bizeljsko-Sremič wine-growing district at the Ribnik Dular guesthouse. There is a rest area for motorhomes.

**Sports and Recreation Center Bazen Brestanica** – a small pool and its surroundings with safe children's playgrounds are intended for the youngest and their parents. The large pool is intended for all generations; a shallower part is suitable for less skilled swimmers, while swimming masters can refresh themselves in the deeper part.

**Famous Slovenes born in Brestanica**: Adam Bohorič and Father Simon Ašič.

**Hiking and Cycling** – arranged hiking trails in the vicinity of Brestanica include Janezova pot, Trapistovska pot, Brestaniška pot, and Pešpot na grad Rajhenburg. Brestanica offers cycling opportunities along the Sava River and around Brestanica; a new addition is a cycling path through a 590 m long tunnel connecting Brestanica from the fire station to the railway station.

**Climbing Wall Armeško** – at the Armeško signpost in Brestanica, turn right from the main road, drive about two kilometers, turn right at the "konec Armeškega" sign, and park immediately. It is a 10-minute walk to the climbing area. The terrain is quite steep but the path is well laid out. Climbing is possible year-round. Protection against ticks and mosquitoes is recommended.
