# The Knights of Rajhenburg

*Vitezi Rajhenburški* | *Die Ritter von Rajhenburg*

The Knights of Rajhenburg is a permanent exhibition at [Grad Rajhenburg](../destinations/grad-rajhenburg.md). It opened on 14 October 2022.

## Exhibition Overview

The exhibition tells the story of the Rajhenburg family’s 429-year history as tenants (*ministeriales*) of the Archbishops of Salzburg. It provides a multi-layered view, expanding its central theme with added information about other nearby castle buildings and their owners, the composition of medieval society, and the lifestyles of its three estates both in the space of present-day [Posavje](../destinations/posavska-statistical-region.md) and the wider area.

## Historical Context

The construction of Rajhenburg Castle between 1131 and 1147 was commissioned by Archbishop of Salzburg Konrad I. It was erected as part of the defence system and settlement of the border area between the Holy Roman Empire and the Kingdom of Hungary, which adopted a final peace treaty in 1131. The *ministeriales* who appeared here during this time derived their family name from Castle Rajhenburg.

## The Rajhenburg Family

Twelve generations came and went between the first mention of Oton I of Rajhenburg and the death of the last male representative of the family, Hans Rajnpreht. 54 male family members and only 16 women are known to us by name.

Rajnpreht I of Rajhenburg holds a special place among them. His likeness in the monumental work *The Triumphal Procession* recorded his place in history as the first knight of Emperor Maximillian I of Habsburg. Rajnpreht and his ancestors and descendants are encountered at numerous estates and in a variety of functions. During their final decades, the family also fundamentally remodelled their primary castle – Rajhenburg.

## Exhibition Development and Design

The exhibition took years and an extensive team to complete. A crucial role was played by Boris Hajdinjak, director of the Centre of Jewish Cultural Heritage Synagogue Maribor and a researcher who dedicated decades to the study of the Middle Ages.

Original museum materials were sourced at:
- The National Gallery of Slovenia
- The National Museum of Slovenia
- The Celje Regional Museum
- The Posavje Museum Brežice

Texts are complemented by numerous reproductions of archival and museum documents, replicas of select pieces of knights’ gear and armament, as well as newly fashioned dress and apparel. The exhibition was further augmented with original drawings and digital representations of materials, as well as interactive content for younger visitors.

## Setting

The exhibition is set in the spaces of the castle's Renaissance section, terminating in a room boasting remarkable Renaissance frescoes. These have been interpreted for the exhibition by Dr Tomislav Vignjević.

## Cultural Heritage Links

The Rajhenburgs are also linked to several units of cultural heritage both locally and in the wider area, including the Beautiful Pietà from Brestanica and the Šrajbarski Turn castle structure.

## Exhibition Credits

- **Exhibition Curator:** Museum Counsellor Dr Helena Rožman, employed at the museum branches of Kulturni Dom Krško, who also coordinated the exhibition project.
- **Spatial Layout and Visual Appearance:** Designer Polona Zupančič.
