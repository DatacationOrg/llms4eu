# Milan Grlj

**Milan Anton Grlj** (5 January 1904 – 24 April 1981) was a Slovene Roman Catholic priest, folk poet, and national activist.

## Life

Milan Grlj was born on 5 January 1904 in Zarečje, near Ilirska Bistrica. His father, Franc, was a railway worker, and his mother, Marija Jeršinovič, was a homemaker. He attended primary school in Trnovo and gymnasium at the Trappist monastery in Rajhenburg (Brestanica). He studied theology in Rijeka and Venice.

He was ordained a priest on 22 June 1929 in Rijeka. He spent most of his priestly life serving in the Diocese of Rijeka, including in Moščenice and Brgud. In 1941, he was confined in Verona, Italy, together with priests Msgr. Karl Jamnik and Janez Kalan. From 1942 to 1973, he served as parish priest in Podgraje near Ilirska Bistrica.

On 6 May 1944, German forces burned Podgraje, destroying the rectory and its inventory. After the war, he rebuilt the parish church of Our Lady of Mount Carmel (with frescoes by Tone Kralj, 1958–59) and the rectory. He also built a new filial church dedicated to Our Lady of Sorrows in Zabiče. After retiring in 1973, he lived at his home in Harije, where he died on 24 April 1981 and is buried.

## Character and Legacy

Grlj was known for his cheerful and sociable character and possessed an exceptional oratorical gift, which he used to encourage people to embrace and preserve religious and national values. He enriched his speech with poetic verses, though he never published his creations. He is remembered for his dedication to divine and human laws.
