**Milan Pertot** (also known as **Pertot Milan**) was a Slovene electrical engineer and technical writer.

## Life and Career

Milan Pertot was born on 20 May 1884 in Trieste (Trst). His father was Simon Pertot, a physician from Barcola (Barkovlje) near Trieste, and his mother was Frančiška Eber. He completed his secondary education at the German gymnasium in Trieste and went on to study at the polytechnic in Prague, where he graduated in 1909.

His first employment was in Prague. He later returned to his native region and, from 1913, served as the director of the tramway operating on the Matulji–Opatija–Lovran line. In 1923, he left the Primorska region and initially worked as the operations manager of the Zagorje ob Savi mine. From 1932, he managed the electrical installations at the Trbovlje coal mine. He participated in the construction of the thermal power plants in Trbovlje and Brestanica. During the Second World War, he was expelled to Sisak in 1941. After the war, he was employed by the Coal Directorate of the People's Republic of Slovenia (LRS).

## Work on Nikola Tesla

Pertot established himself as a technical writer in both domestic and foreign press. He dedicated the majority of his publishing activities to Nikola Tesla, with the desire that both the domestic and international public would recognize the reality of Tesla's discoveries. These efforts culminated in his book *Nikola Tesla – pionir elektrotehniške dobe* (Nikola Tesla – Pioneer of the Electrotechnical Age), published in Ljubljana in 1962.

## Death

Milan Pertot died in February 1967 in Ljubljana.
