**Maksimilijan Držečnik** (born **Maksimilijan Luka Držečnik**, also known as **Maks Držečnik**; 5 October 1903, Zgornja Orlica – 13 May 1978, Ljubljana) was a Slovenian Roman Catholic theologian, pastoral sociologist, and Bishop of Maribor.

## Early Life and Education

Born to Luka Držečnik, a farmer, mayor, and soldier, and Roza Držečnik (née Osvald). His brother was Janko Držečnik, a surgeon.

After graduating from the classical gymnasium in Maribor, he entered the Maribor seminary in 1924. In 1927, he began studies at the Faculty of Philosophy of the Pontifical Gregorian University in Rome, completing them in 1930, and continued with theology at the same university. His classmates included future cardinals Joseph Höffner, Franz König, Alojzij Stepinac, Franjo Šeper, and Bishop Josef Köstner. He was ordained a priest in 1932.

## Early Ministry and World War II

Upon returning from Rome, he served as a chaplain in Ribnica na Pohorju (1933–34) and Celje (1934–36). From 1936, he was chaplain in Svetinje and simultaneously prefect of the diocesan minor seminary in Maribor. In 1936, he began lecturing in moral philosophy at the Maribor Theological School, and the following year was appointed docent for the Old Testament. In November 1938, he defended his dissertation *Doctrina s. Ambrosii de Christo Deo-Homine* at the Gregorian University. He was appointed vice-rector of the Maribor seminary in 1938, a position he held until its dissolution in April 1941 at the start of World War II.

His thorough patristic, dogmatic, and biblical studies in Rome, combined with excellent knowledge of French, Italian, and German, gave him broad access to contemporary European theological currents that urged both clergy and laity to committed and continuous study of the Holy Scripture.

When the Germans occupied Maribor, Držečnik was among the first priests arrested on 21 April 1941. He spent several months in German prisons, initially detained in the Maribor barracks, then transferred to the Trappist monastery in Rajhenburg (present-day [Brestanica](../destinations/brestanica)). On 5 July, he was exiled to Croatia, where Archbishop Stepinac received the priests and assigned them to parishes in his diocese. Držečnik was appointed spiritual assistant in the parish of Visoko near Novi Marof in Hrvaško Zagorje, where he remained until the end of the war.

## Bishop of Maribor

After the war, students of the Lavantine theological school and seminary (both abolished by the Germans in 1941) were accepted in Ljubljana, and Držečnik became their superior. In August 1946, he was appointed docent for the Old Testament, and in September 1946, auxiliary bishop of Lavant in Maribor. The communist authorities long refused to recognize him as bishop. After the death of Bishop Ivan Jožef Tomažič in February 1949, he assumed leadership of the diocese as apostolic administrator, remaining in this role until 1960, when he was appointed residential bishop. This long period of unresolved episcopal appointment reflects the complex relations between the Church and the post-war authorities, as well as certain tensions and mistrust between the Vatican and Maribor. Držečnik was often criticized for an overly conciliatory attitude toward the authorities, particularly excessive sympathy for priests organized in the Cyril and Methodius Society.

Under the pontificate of Pope John XXIII (1958–63), tensions began to ease. In 1962, a Vatican decree renamed the Diocese of Lavant to the Diocese of Maribor-Lavant and adjusted diocesan boundaries, a change also credited to Držečnik. In 1964, the borders were territorially aligned with the state borders of Yugoslavia, Austria, and Hungary, formally incorporating neighboring territories (the Meža Valley with Dravograd from the Diocese of Gurk, Prekmurje from the Diocese of Szombathely, and three parishes – Kapla, Sveti Duh, and Apače – from the Diocese of Graz-Seckau) that Držečnik (and his predecessors Andrej Karlin and Tomažič) had already administered as apostolic administrator.

## Slovenian Translation of the Bible

One of Držečnik's greatest achievements was the publication of a new Slovenian translation of the Bible. Between 1959 and 1961, under the patronage of the Maribor diocesan ordinariate, the complete *Sveto pismo* was published in four volumes, with 30,000 copies printed. The edition was based on the most modern Catholic Jerusalem translation and included commentaries and historical-critical notes.

## Second Vatican Council and Pastoral Sociology

Držečnik was the only Slovenian bishop to attend all four sessions of the Second Vatican Council, a highlight of his ministry. He actively participated and delivered a notable address in September 1965, presenting the need for the Church to prepare for action in changed social and cultural conditions through the study of the sociology of religion. This effort contributed, both globally and domestically, to the introduction of new pastoral structures and influenced the wording of subsequent conciliar documents, especially the Pastoral Constitution on the Church in the Modern World.

As early as 1959, Bishop Držečnik was the first Yugoslav bishop to establish a Council for Pastoral Sociology in his diocese, which developed a detailed plan for sociological research within parishes and began compiling religious and pastoral terminology. He strongly encouraged lay participation in church communities and promoted the establishment of parish pastoral councils.

## Ecumenism

He also devoted himself to ecumenical issues. At the Second Vatican Council, he presented Anton Martin Slomšek as an apostle of spiritual ecumenism. As president of the ecumenical commission of the Yugoslav Bishops' Conference, he decisively contributed to the first ecumenical document *Prenova in sprava* (Renewal and Reconciliation, 1974), which significantly advanced the rapprochement of Christian churches in Yugoslavia. In 1974, he organized the first Yugoslav ecumenical symposium in Maribor.

## Other Achievements

Other significant successes of his episcopal efforts include the establishment of the "minor seminary" (1961, now Slomšek Diocesan Minor Seminary), the revival of theological studies in 1968, and the construction of the Theological Seminary, all in Maribor, as well as care for building new churches and pastoral centers (Maribor, Tezno, Odranci, Rače, Senovo). He was among the initiators for the establishment of the Slovenian ecclesiastical province (Metropolitanate of Ljubljana) in 1968. He ensured the completion of the diocesan process for the beatification of Slomšek and had the collected documentation transferred to Rome for review by the Vatican Congregation for the Causes of Saints. He strove for unity among Christians while simultaneously achieving consensus with both ecclesiastical and state authorities.

## Honors and Legacy

In 1967, he received the Order of the Republic with a Silver Wreath. A memorial plaque was unveiled on his birth house in 1993, a statue erected in Ribnica na Pohorju in 2003, and a street in central Maribor was named after him in 2011.
