Franc Jamšek (17 July 1840 – 22 July 1892) was a Slovenian educator, school supervisor, and pedagogical writer.

## Life and Career

Born in Žalec, he completed his local primary school and was admitted to the third grade of the Celje main school in 1854. He passed the teaching examination for trivial schools at the Celje teacher training college in 1857. The Lavant consistory appointed him as an assistant teacher in Konjice, then at Sv. Križ pri Slatini (1860), Sv. Rok ob Sotli, and Mozirje (1863–1868).

In 1868, he attended the first agricultural course in Vienna. He served for one year at the main school in Celje, and in 1869 obtained a teaching, sexton, and organist position in Loka pri Zidanem Mostu. From 1870 until his death, he was the head teacher in Rajhenburg (now [Brestanica](../destinations/brestanica)). Concurrently, from 1869 to 1875, he served as the school supervisor for the Brežice, Kozjansko, and Sevnica school districts. In recognition of his successful work, he received the title of school director in 1888.

As district school supervisor during the transition of public education from the Concordat era to new conditions, he ensured the election of teacher representatives to the new district school councils, accustomed teachers to new state and provincial school laws, and helped draft new curricula.

He was a very active chairman of the Teachers' Association of the Brežice District Administration from its founding in 1872. At various teacher meetings, he lectured on the need for and methodology of geography lessons in primary schools. He joined the Pedagogical Society in Krško, was a board member, and co-editor of the *Pedagoški letnik* (from 1888).

## Writings and Pedagogical Views

In the *Pedagoški letnik*, he published a discussion on the causes of coarseness among out-of-school youth and means to remedy this sad phenomenon (1887). From his diary, he recorded *Spomini na Dunaj* (Memories of Vienna, 1889), incorporating sketches on the development of Styrian and Austrian schooling from 1867 to 1870.

He advocated for the professional reputation of teachers among the people, the regulation of teacher salaries, and wrote on school discipline. He held that the teaching profession should be the center of a teacher's activity. He gave advice to teacher trainees, addressed why some mentally normal children do not achieve the prescribed learning goals, how to promote uniform class progress, and means for promoting morality among youth.

On methodological questions, he wrote under the collective heading *Iz šole za šolo* (From School for School) about composition procedures and published composition and spelling exercises, as well as on the use of punctuation and singing instruction.

He highly esteemed Anton Martin Slomšek, writing an extensive discussion about him and publishing *Šolske drobtinice* (School Crumbs, Ljubljana 1887) for the 25th anniversary of his death. In it, he described Slomšek as a pedagogue, patriot, and nationalist, presenting his views on home education, schools and teachers, mother tongue, and singing, and added methodological discussions on sentence formation and Slovenian orthography.

Based on the German *Der Irrgarten der Erziehung*, he adapted *Napake pri vzgoji otrok v zverižosteznem vrtu* (Mistakes in Child Rearing in the Maze Garden), published as a supplement to *Popotnik* in 1890 and separately in Maribor in 1891. It instructs parents on what they should not do in raising children.

He also corresponded with *Slovenski narod* on local events, school decisions, and teacher matters. He used the pseudonyms Žalski, Žalčan, and Žavski.

## Other Activities

As a conscientious youth educator, he taught agriculture, especially viticulture, and collaborated with the agricultural branch in Sevnica and various cultural and national societies.
