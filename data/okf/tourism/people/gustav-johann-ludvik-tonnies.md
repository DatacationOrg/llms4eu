Gustav Johann Ludvik Tönnies (also known as Gustav Tönnies or Gustav Johann Ludwig Tönnies) was born on 16 January 1814 in Stralsund, Pomerania (Germany), to an immigrant ship carpenter from Småland, Sweden. He died on 12 November 1886 in Ljubljana.

## Early Life and Training

Tönnies trained as a carpenter. From 1830 to 1839, he traveled as a carpenter's journeyman through Germany, Austria, Switzerland, and France, and also visited Sweden, Norway, and Russia.

## Career in Ljubljana

In 1842, he worked for Ohmeier as a carpenter foreman in Graz, also engaging in building locksmithing. When the Graz contractor J. B. Withalm built the Kolizej building in Ljubljana in 1845, Tönnies was tasked with constructing its roof truss. He decided to remain in Ljubljana. In 1846, he passed an examination at the provincial building directorate, gaining recognition as a qualified city master carpenter, and obtained a trade license from the magistrate.

Tönnies did not limit himself to carpentry. Exploiting favorable economic conditions, including the construction of the Southern Railway through Ljubljana, he expanded his enterprise to the entire construction sector, initially focusing on railway and industrial buildings. In 1858, he built a steam sawmill with a machine joinery and parquet workshop on what is now Titova cesta. To secure building materials, he purchased quarries in Nabrežina, Momjan, and Repentabor, and owned a brickyard in Koseze near Ljubljana, which he equipped in 1883 with a circular kiln, the first in the former Carniola.

In 1871, he partnered with F. Dobner, former director of the ironworks in Dvor near Žužemberk, and established, alongside building locksmithing, an iron foundry and machine workshop. Dobner left in 1880, and Tönnies remained the sole owner of the firm "G. T. strojna delavnica in železolivarna" (G. T. Machine Workshop and Iron Foundry).

## Major Construction Projects

Tönnies executed works and station buildings on the Southern Railway stations (Celje–Trieste). In 1870–71, he performed all carpentry, joinery, and locksmithing work on the Upper Carniola railway (Ljubljana–Tarvisio). He renovated the burned-down sugar refinery on Poljanski nasip in 1858. In 1866, he built the Kosler brewery in Šiška. In 1871, he built the former Tschinkel & Son candy factory on today's Titova cesta. He expanded the Ljubljana spinning mill. He participated in the construction of the tobacco factory in Ljubljana (1872–73), port warehouses in Trieste, and the expansion and reconstruction of the Trieste railway station, among other projects.

## Industrial Manufacturing

Tönnies was also significant in the industrial sector, manufacturing machines for the wood industry and crafts, suction devices, turbines, and steam engines. At the time of his death, approximately 650 people were employed in all his operations.

## Family and Succession

Tönnies married Amalija Malovrh from Ljubljana. His sons took over the businesses:

- **Gustav Tönnies Jr.** (3 September 1851 – 22 October 1922) completed the Ljubljana Realschule and studied construction at the Vienna School of Arts and Crafts. In 1887, he obtained the right to practice as a master builder (later also stonemasonry and carpentry) in Trieste, where he worked as a builder until 1900. In 1901, he received a concession for the same trades in Ljubljana and replaced his deceased brother Adolf as the holder of the trade license for the construction firm.
- **Adolf Tönnies** (21 September 1855 – 13 June 1900) attended the Realschule in Ljubljana (1867–70) and qualified as a builder. He carried out construction work for his father's company on the Southern Railway in Trieste. In 1883, he passed the master's examination at the technical department of the governorate and obtained a concession for Trieste and, in the same year, for Ljubljana, serving as the license holder for the G. T. enterprise until his death.
- **Viljem Tönnies** (24 April 1857 – 31 May 1925) completed three grades of the Realschule in Ljubljana, trained in machine construction, and held the technical management of the iron foundry and machine workshop.
- **Emil Tönnies** (7 September 1863 – 30 August 1941) completed two grades of the Realschule in Ljubljana, became a businessman, and managed his father's quarries in Nabrežina and Repentabor before World War I, later taking over the commercial management of "Gradbeno podjetje G. T. nasl." (G. T. Successors Construction Company), a limited liability company that continued the construction business of the former Tönnies firm.

## Later Company Activities

Towards the end of the 19th century, the company significantly increased its activity. In 1896, it carried out renovation and reconstruction work on the Church of Sts. Peter and James (damaged in the 1895 earthquake). It built a number of important public buildings, including the Opera House (1892), the military hospital on Zaloška cesta (1897), several buildings for the provincial hospital, the courthouse, the gymnasium in Prežihova ulica (1899), the jubilee poorhouse (1899), the city lyceum (1906), and Mladika (1906). Private buildings included the house at Tavčarjeva ulica 2 (Hribarjeva), the house at Miklošičeva cesta 16 (Bambergova), both designed by architect M. Fabiani, the house at Trubarjeva cesta 2, and residential buildings at Gradišče 8 and 10 for the pension fund of the Carniolan Savings Bank. Outside Ljubljana, the company built, among others, the church in Bled (1900) and the church in Brestanica (1908).

## Post-World War I Changes

New economic conditions after World War I and subjective reasons led to changes in the Tönnies enterprises. In 1919, the iron foundry and machine workshop were sold to the newly established joint-stock company Strojne tovarne in livarne (which also took over M. Samassa and Avg. Žabkar). The construction business was continued by the Tönnies family in two newly founded companies: G. T. nasl., družba z o. z. (G. T. Successors, Ltd.) and Obnova, gradbena družba z o. z. (Obnova, Construction Company Ltd.). Both were led by the youngest son, engineer Rudolf Tönnies, a builder (20 April 1869 – 6 December 1929 in Munich), who also studied civil engineering. He worked as an engineer for the Croatian government in Zagreb and then as a leading architect for the government of Bosnia and Herzegovina in Sarajevo. He returned to Ljubljana in 1918, obtained a trade concession as a master builder, and joined his brothers. In Ljubljana, he built the palace of the Ljubljana Credit Bank (now the seat of the National Bank) around 1923 and, at the same time, the Ljubljana Court (seat of the Railway Transport Company).
