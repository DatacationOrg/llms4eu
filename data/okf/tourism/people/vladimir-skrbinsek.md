## Biography

Vladimir Skrbinšek (born 2 October 1902 in Ljubljana, died 1987) was a Slovenian actor, director, and theatre pedagogue. He was the brother of Milan Skrbinšek.

### Education

He attended primary school from 1908 to 1910 at Sv. Marjeta on the Drava Plain (Dravsko polje), then in Ljubljana from 1910 to 1912. He completed secondary school (Realka) in Ljubljana from 1912 to 1919, where he passed his Matura in 1919. He then studied electrical engineering for four semesters from 1919 to 1921.

### Early Career

From 1921 to 1923, he worked as a clerk for the state railways. During this period, he began acting and directing in amateur productions at Pragersko, Ruše, and Rajhenburg (Brestanica).

### Theatre Career

Skrbinšek made his professional debut in 1923 at both the Ljubljana and Maribor Drama theatres, ultimately choosing to join the Slovenian National Theatre (SNG) in Maribor on 1 July 1923.

His subsequent engagements included:
- 1924–1925: Varaždin
- 1 January – 1 August 1925: Cabaret Klub and a travelling theatre in Zagreb
- 1925–1927: Split
- 1927–1935: Maribor
- 1935–1938: Skopje
- 1 August 1938 – 31 January 1941: Maribor
- 1 February – 30 April 1941: Skopje
- 1 May 1941 – 31 August 1957: Ljubljana (with a sick leave from 1947 to 1949)
- 1957–1960: Director of the Drama Theatre in Maribor
- From 1960: Member of the City Theatre (Mestno gledališče) in Ljubljana, while also appearing as a guest at the SNG Drama.

He received the Prešeren Award in 1962 and 1965.

### Roles and Directing

By 1963, Skrbinšek had performed over 350 roles of various sizes, including most of the major ones. In Maribor and Skopje, he also performed in opera and operetta, singing around 50 parts and directing over 30 productions.

He developed into one of the most distinctive character and intellectual actors. His notable roles included:
- **Classical drama:** Mercutio, Tybalt, Iago, Hamlet, Malvolio, the Fool in *King Lear*, Richard III, Henry IV, Mosca and Voltore (Ben Jonson's *Volpone*), Cyrano (Rostand).
- **Molière:** Chrysalde (*The School for Wives*), Philinte, Alceste (*The Misanthrope*), Tartuffe.
- **Ibsen and Shaw:** Gregers Werle (*The Wild Duck*), Cocane (*How to Get Rich*), Colenso Ridgeon (*The Doctor's Dilemma*), Dudgeon (*The Devil's Disciple*), Professor Higgins (*Pygmalion*), Sir Warwick, the Inquisitor (*Saint Joan*), and Shaw himself in Kilty's *Dear Liar*.
- **Other international works:** Lord Darlington (*Lady Windermere's Fan*), Lord Goring (*An Ideal Husband* by Wilde), Vershinin (*Three Sisters* by Chekhov), von Gerlach (*The Condemned of Altona* by Sartre), Dr. Oppenheimer (Lipphardt).
- **Slovenian drama:** The husband of Dr. Grozd, Dr. Gruden, Maks, Ščuka, Zlodej, Hlapec Jernej, the priest in *Hlapci* (Cankar); Herman Celjski (Novačan); Friderik, Pravdač (Kreft, *Celjski grofje*); Tahi (*Velika puntarija*); Baron Zois (*Kranjski komedijanti*).
- **Krleža:** Leone (*The Glembays*), Baron Lenbach and Dr. Križevec (*Agony*), Roul Sigurd Morgens (*Aretej*).

### Film Career

Skrbinšek appeared in the first Slovenian film, *Trst* (as a German officer), and in other films including *Jara gospoda* (as Tine pl. Mali), *Trobenta in oblak* (as a German officer), *Samorastniki* (as Karničnik), and *Zarota* (as Prof. Dahling).

### Pedagogy

In Skopje (1936–1937), he was engaged as a teacher at the opera school of the music institute, where he staged *Cavalleria Rusticana* and excerpts from *Faust* and *Carmen* with his students. In Maribor (1 September 1938 – 1 January 1941), he led a free school for dramatic acting and directing, training several prominent actors and directors, including J. Babič, H. Leskovšek, B. Brunčko, F. Kumer, J. Lukež, and St. Ledinek. He also staged plays with grammar school and commercial academy students.

### Writing

He wrote articles for theatre programmes and newspapers on artistic and organisational questions, particularly about the premieres he directed.
