**Dioniz Stur** (also known as **Dionýz Štúr**, **Dionys Stur**, **Dioniz Štur**; 2 April 1827 – 9 October 1893) was a Slovak geologist, paleontologist, and paleobotanist. He was the director of the Imperial Geological Institute (*Geologische Reichsanstalt*) in Vienna.

## Early Life and Education

Stur was born on 2 April 1827 in Beckov, Slovakia (then part of the Kingdom of Hungary), to Jožef, a teacher-cantor at an evangelical school, and Johana (née Rizner). His older brother, Ludvik, was a Slovak writer and national revivalist.

He attended gymnasium in Modra (Modern), completed a philosophy course at the Evangelical Lyceum in Bratislava, and from 1844 studied mathematics and physics at the Polytechnic in Vienna. From 1847, he specialized in mineralogy and geognosy at the Montanist Museum, and furthered his studies with a scholarship at the Mining Academy in Štiavnica (Schemnitz).

## Career

Due to the unrest of 1848–1849, Stur returned to Vienna. In 1849, at the invitation of Wilhelm Haidinger, the first director of the Imperial Geological Institute, he joined the newly founded institute alongside geologist [Marko Lipold](people/marko-lipold). He became its director in 1885 and led the institute until his retirement in 1893.

### Geological Mapping

Between 1850 and 1872, Stur geologically mapped almost all parts of the Austro-Hungarian Empire for a general geological map. In the summer of 1856, he researched Upper Carniola (Gorenjska), Inner Carniola (Notranjska), and the Gorizia (Goriška) region.

From 1863 to 1865, commissioned by the Geognostic-Montanistic Society for Styria, he revised and supplemented the results of geological mapping carried out by geologists and commissioners of the society, including A. von Morlot, K. J. Andrae, F. Rolle, and T. Zollikofer. In 1864, he surveyed the Slovene part of Styria. He published the *Geologische Uebersichtskarte des Herzogthums Steiermark* (Graz, 1865) and an extensive commentary in the book *Geologie der Steiermark* (Graz, 1871), which served as a foundation for further research for many years.

### Research in Slovene Lands

At the invitation of the Styrian provincial governor, Stur investigated the hydrological conditions in Rogaška Slatina. He researched the geological conditions in the vicinity of Rablje (Cave del Predil) and Mrzla voda. At the initiative of Marko Lipold, then director of the Idrija mine, he studied the strata in the Idrija area in more detail and determined the fauna discovered there.

Stur determined and partly described a series of fossils from various geological periods and various sites in Slovenia, including:
- Sava near Jesenice in Upper Carniola
- Kamna Gora near Konjice in Styria
- Raštajn near [Brestanica](destinations/brestanica) (Rajhenburg)
- Legen near Slovenj Gradec
- Trbovlje
- Govce

### Paleobotany

Stur was among the few paleontologists who also successfully worked in the field of paleobotany. He published several botanical works in his youth, one of which demonstrated how geological soil conditions influence vegetation. He processed the Tertiary flora from the Soteska strata near Motnik, where he discovered two new fern species: *Aspidium trinkeri* and *Osmunda grutschreiberi*. From the Sava Caves under Golica, he determined a larger number of Upper Carboniferous plant species.

### Other Contributions

After the 1870 earthquake that severely affected Klana in northern Istria and damaged various places in Slovenia, Stur attempted to explain the origin of the earthquake through the geological and tectonic structure of the wider area, extending far into Slovene territory. He published several reports from earthquake observers from various places in Slovenia and his expert opinion *Zur Wasserversorgungs-Frage d. Landeshauptstadt Laibach* (Vienna, 1887).

## Death

Stur died on 9 October 1893 in Vienna.
