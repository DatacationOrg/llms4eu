# Scraper arena — research log

Which HTML→markdown extractor should the pipeline use, and is our wrapper around
trafilatura earning its keep?

Sixteen working sessions, 2026-08-18 to 2026-08-21: setup and the judging system,
the flag audit, hunk-level review, the fetcher re-measured at the markdown layer,
agreement statistics corrected, the corpus pinned to immutable revisions, and the
committed-vs-branch comparison. Numbers below are measured, not estimated;
where something was claimed but not verified it says so. Where an earlier number
in this file was wrong, the correction says what it was and why.

Live results live in `.local/scraper-arena/` (gitignored) and are regenerated by
`just arena-report`. This file records decisions and findings, not generated output.

## Premise — stated, not tested

**Cleaner markdown makes a better corpus.** Quality is the objective; speed is a
constraint we watch. Whether cleaner markdown actually improves downstream retrieval
is **explicitly out of scope** — the repo has the harness for it (`just eval`,
`eval-index`, and `src/scraping/extract_markdown.py` is the production extractor
this arena's `ours` mirrors), and it was a deliberate decision not to spend the
study there.

This is a declared assumption, not an oversight, and every result below inherits
it. A reader who rejects the premise should read the whole log as a measurement of
markdown cleanliness and nothing more.

## Setup

### Corpus stability (session 4) — the input is now immutable

Raised by the reviewer while labelling: the corpus mixed pages whose content turns
over constantly with pages that do not, the reference image had drifted from the
text being judged, and preference voting on news prose is subjective in a way that
makes a vote hard to justify. All three were real.

- **Wikipedia is pinned to `?oldid=` permalinks.** All 64 wiki candidates in
  `corpus/sites.yaml` carry an explicit revision id; 50 are selected. `/wiki/Title?oldid=N`
  was chosen over `/w/index.php?title=&oldid=N` because it keeps `/wiki/` in the
  path, so existing title/`is_wiki` parsing keeps working. Verified: the pinned form
  serves the frozen revision (`wgRevisionId` matches), the "old revision" banner
  MediaWiki adds is dropped by trafilatura — 0 leaked lines — and the extract differs
  from the live page only by the revision's own content.
- **`fetch_wikitext` forwards that `oldid`.** It previously used bare `action=raw`,
  so wikiextractor read whatever revision was current when that pass ran — a median
  **44 minutes** after `raw.html`. Checked against the MediaWiki revisions API:
  **0 of 50** articles were edited inside that window, so the wiki three-way was
  never actually contaminated. Luck, not design, and it cannot recur.
- **Page ids are `uuid5(NAMESPACE_URL, url)`, so pinning creates new pages.** The 50
  unpinned wiki pages are marked `dropped=1, drop_reason='superseded by pinned
  ?oldid= revision'`; the 169 votes referencing them (73 human, 96 panel) plus 16
  hunk votes were archived to `votes.retired-unpinned-wiki.<stamp>.jsonl` and removed
  from the live table, so ratings describe one corpus version. 161 votes on the
  untouched non-wiki pages survive. Pre-change DB kept as `arena.db.bak-prepin`.
  `snapshot.py` gained `--buckets` so a full re-fetch pass never has to invalidate
  those 161 votes as collateral again.

### Screenshots retired (session 4)

The reference image was a median **221 minutes** newer than the HTML being judged,
so on any page that turns over it showed content present in neither column. It is
gone: `--no-screenshots` on the snapshot pass, `verify()` no longer requires the
variant, and the arena serves a **live iframe** instead — safe *because* the wiki
slice is pinned (a live iframe of a frozen revision is the snapshot). Where a site
refuses framing, the pane falls back to the saved DOM, i.e. the variant the
extractors actually read.

**Frameability is measured, not guessed** (`snapshots.frameable`, from
`X-Frame-Options` / CSP `frame-ancestors` at fetch time): **69 of 100 pages
frameable, 31 not**. All 6 Wikipedia hosts allow it; refusers are almost exactly the
volatile news/government pages (BBC, Guardian, tagesschau, NOS, KNMI, kormany.hu,
gov.si, MDN, arXiv). For the 50 non-wiki pages the flag was backfilled with a
header-only request, deliberately not re-fetching bodies, so votes stayed valid.

Also fixed: the page arena's reference link omitted `variant`, so `/snapshot`
defaulted to `rendered` — a document the extractors were not given on 97 of 100
pages. Session 2 had fixed this for the hunk arena and never carried it across.

### Default route swapped back to the page arena (session 4)

`/` serves the page arena again; the hunk arena moved to `/hunks`. Session 2's
hunk-first choice suited comparing two *configurations of one extractor* (mostly
identical text); the work now is cross-library comparison over a freshly pinned
corpus where outputs differ throughout, so the whole output is the unit judged.

### Whole-document diff marking, and making a 90%-identical pair readable (session 4)

Requested while labelling: keep the whole output, but mark which parts belong to
only one side (`hunks.mark`), rather than replacing the document with only the
differing regions as the hunk arena's `hunks.align` does — wrong when the reviewer
must judge the complete extraction. Reassembling the marked spans returns the
input byte-for-byte on every matchup shape; differences are marked at **word
level** inside related lines, since every real defect in this corpus is sub-line
(a lost space after a citation marker, a zero-padded sort key), so marking a whole
1,200-character Wikipedia paragraph would be correct and useless — the
`notes.[79] Dorothy` case marks exactly one character, the space. Relatedness is
scored on non-whitespace tokens only: scoring all tokens let two unrelated lines
("alpha beta gamma" vs "nothing alike here") pass the 0.40 threshold on shared
spaces alone, marking every word and leaving spaces clean — read as damage rather
than difference, caught by a test, not review. Cost measured before shipping: 7 ms
mean over all 990 adjacent pairs in the corpus, 59 ms worst case on the 716k-char
Project Gutenberg page (431 regions, 12,740 spans).

First pass left the document intact and the reviewer's verdict was "still
unreadable" — pairs here are commonly 90%+ identical. Four more defects, three
found by measurement: **collapsing was a no-op** (identical runs came out as one
span *per line* — 4,002 spans for a 4,000-line document; a coalescing pass took
median text shown from 82% of the document to **5%**, span count to a median of
**17 per side**); **one-sided regions were invisible** on the empty side (fixed
with a "(nothing on this side)" marker); **plain-text entrants had no source
navigation** (`resiliparse`, `justext`, `goose3`, `html-text` emit no ATX headings
— **0 of 13** regions anchored on `resiliparse` vs `resiliparse@rendered`; still
50% of pairs unanchored corpus-wide, inherent); **performance, twice** (section
trails made marking quadratic, 59ms → 1,364ms on the slowest pair, fixed with a
one-pass index; then `_refine` profiled at 96%, fixed by trimming common
prefix/suffix and lowering the middle-diff cap from 3,000 to **400 tokens**).
Final, over all **5,519 pairs** the sampler can serve: **median 1.6ms, p95 26ms,
max 339ms**, byte-exact reassembly held throughout.

### The volatile 20 are kept

20 of 100 pages are news/listing/tourism shapes, all in the `*-other` buckets,
**0 in wiki**. Not dropped: the listing branch is the wrapper's only remaining
contribution and only fires there, so removing them would delete the evidence for
the one piece of our own code kept. The reference-drift complaint is answered by
the saved-DOM fallback, not by shrinking the corpus. The 4×25 bucket design is
unchanged.

- **Corpus**: 100 pages, 4 buckets of 25 — `en-wiki`, `en-other`, `eu-wiki`,
  `eu-other`. Non-English: nl, de, sl, hu, es, pl. Selected from ~140 candidates in
  `corpus/sites.yaml`; first 25 per bucket that fetch cleanly.
- Page **shape** varied deliberately (biography, infobox, table-heavy, listing,
  gov, docs, blog, SPA…) — extractors diverge by shape far more than by topic.
- **Fetch once, extract many**: each page saved as `raw.html` (httpx),
  `rendered.html` (Playwright), `wikitext.txt` (wiki only), `screenshot.jpg`.
  robots.txt honoured, per-domain throttle.

## The decision (session 5) — locked in

**`httpx` (raw HTML, no browser) + `trafilatura` driven by our fallback ladder.**

### Speed, mean ms per page, 100 live pages

Extraction timings are warmup + 3 timed runs per cell; fetch timings are per-request.

| pipeline | fetch | extract | total | markdown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:--|
| httpx + resiliparse | 1058 | 9 | 1067 | no |
| httpx + html-text | 1058 | 26 | 1085 | no |
| httpx + justext | 1058 | 64 | 1122 | no |
| httpx + readability-lxml | 1058 | 159 | 1217 | via markdownify |
| httpx + trafilatura | 1058 | 174 | 1233 | native |
| httpx + scrapling-md | 1058 | 183 | 1242 | native |
| **httpx + ours** | 1058 | 269 | **1328** | native |
| httpx + goose3 | 1058 | 603 | 1662 | no |

**The extractor choice is not a speed decision.** Fetch (1058 ms) dominates the
whole extractor field (594 ms span end to end). Trading our extractor for the
fastest on the board saves 260 ms — 20% of the pipeline — and gives up all markdown
structure.

The speed decision that *does* matter is rendering: Playwright costs
**3178 ms/page** against httpx's 1058 — three times the entire pipeline — and the
`@rendered` variants never beat their raw counterparts in either vote pool.
Rendering stays a per-page fallback.

### Why the ladder, and not stock trafilatura

Comparing `ours` against stock `trafilatura` across **97 pages**, ignoring
blank-line differences: **89 identical, 8 substantively different.** Seven of the
eight are listing-shaped (`listing` ×3, `gov-listing` ×2, `institutional-listing`,
`tourism`). On those the ladder rescues rather than merely differs:

| page | ours | stock trafilatura |
|---|---|---|
| `rtve.es/noticias/` | `# Noticias…` + full linked index, 7 479 ch | one unrelated radio blurb, **311 ch** |
| `visitljubljana.com` | `# Visit Ljubljana` + linked listing, 2 498 ch | weather widget ("29°C Wednesday…"), 6 628 ch |
| `rtvslo.si` | `# RTV SLO` + linked headlines, 5 230 ch | search placeholder ("Ni najdenih zadetkov"), 6 941 ch |
| `smithsonianmag.com/history/` | title + linked articles, 7 401 ch | one arbitrary article, 3 842 ch |
| `kormany.hu`, `knmi.nl` | title + linked listing | one article / one intro sentence |

Rows 2–3: stock trafilatura emitted **more characters**, and they were furniture —
character count inverts the true ranking there, the concrete reason the rubric's
first criterion is "was the data found", not "was more text kept".

So the ladder is a no-op on 89 of 97 pages and buys correct extraction on
listing/homepage shapes for +95 ms. That is the whole justification for keeping it.

### A measurement we do NOT have

An earlier draft nearly reported Scrapy at 3757 ms/page against httpx's 297 ms.
**Invalid**: `fetch_scrapy` runs a batch crawl at `CONCURRENT_REQUESTS: 4` with the
per-request timer started at *queue* time, so most of that figure is queue wait.
There is no valid Scrapy-vs-httpx speed measurement here, and the pick doesn't rest
on one — Scrapy is content-equivalent, so there's no reason to switch away from
what we ship.

### Ruled out, and why

- `resiliparse`, `html-text`, `justext`, `goose3` — 10–30× faster, but plain text
  only, cannot express a table or heading — a criterion-1b failure on every
  structured page, most of the corpus.
- `scrapling-md` — leads the LLM pool but sits 7th in the human pool on 4 votes, so
  the lead is sampling noise. Also inherits the Scrapling fetch-layer defect (later
  shown not to apply as tested — see corrections below).
- `readability-lxml` — genuinely close, 110 ms cheaper. Not chosen because its
  markdown is *ours* (markdownify over its HTML), one more layer to own. Nothing in
  the votes separates it from trafilatura.

### What would have been needed to do this properly

Written down because the pick is being locked in on less:

- **Thousands of pages, not 100** — enough to rank tools, not to certify one.
- **~20–30 votes per pair.** We have ~1.5. No two extractors are separated at 95%
  confidence in either pool; every one of the 55 pairs has overlapping intervals.
- **A human self-consistency replicate.** Never run — no estimate of how much judge
  disagreement is noise vs signal.
- **Concurrent throughput measurement.** Everything here is single-threaded on one
  machine, exactly the regime where Scrapy is least favoured.
- **crawl4ai, markitdown, docling.** Never benchmarked at this point (open item 0).

## The challenger round (session 6) — crawl4ai, markitdown, docling

Open item 0 half done: the three libraries registered, run over the whole corpus,
votable. **Votes not yet cast** at this point — structural evidence only.

### They run offline, and only their converter competes

All three read the same saved `raw.html` as the other twelve. Matters most for
`crawl4ai` (fetcher *and* extractor): letting it fetch its own copy would confound
the two layers, and the fetch-layer result already shows that confound is worth
44% of pages. So `crawl4ai` runs through `DefaultMarkdownGenerator` +
`PruningContentFilter`, never `AsyncWebCrawler`; `docling` needs no model weights
for HTML (`HTMLDocumentBackend` alone). Both run out of process
(`extractors/challenger_worker.py`, `uv run --no-project --with <tool>`) because
`crawl4ai` pulls 97 packages and neither can join `arena-env`.

### We audited their flags, because we audited our own

`crawl4ai`'s default `PruningContentFilter` keeps `Jump to content` and every nav
block:

| `PruningContentFilter` config | output on Ada Lovelace | nav in head |
|---|---:|---|
| default | 126 921 ch | yes |
| `threshold=0.48 dynamic` | 118 997 ch | yes |
| `threshold=0.5 fixed` | 123 058 ch | yes |
| `+ min_word_threshold=50` | 25 667 ch | no |
| `threshold=0.6 dynamic + min_word=30` | 31 871 ch | no |

trafilatura on the same page: 67 891 ch. **No setting both removes navigation and
keeps the article** — `min_word_threshold` buys nav removal at ~62% content cost.
The entrant is scored on defaults; tuning doesn't rescue it, just moves the failure.

### Structural metrics, medians, before any vote

| metric | trafilatura | markitdown | crawl4ai | docling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **wiki, n=50** | | | | |
| chars | 79 629 | 260 574 | 161 722 | 255 077 |
| table rows | 32 | 74 | 78 | 63 |
| headings | 32 | 35 | 33 | 35 |
| bare URLs | 0 | 2 | 28 | 28 |
| nav markers | 0 | 4 | 2 | 1 |
| **non-wiki, n=47** | | | | |
| chars | 2 658 | 22 741 | 9 391 | 15 760 |
| headings | 1 | 14 | 11 | 14 |

Two readings, the second is the trap: (1) challengers keep 2–3× more text and more
furniture (bare URLs, nav markers near-zero only for trafilatura); (2) their higher
table-row counts are **not extra coverage** — on `en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam`,
trafilatura captures the infobox (mayor ✓, area ✓) in 83 rows / 134 664 chars;
`docling` captures the same infobox in 152 rows / **553 644 chars**, surplus being
revision-history rows, image alt-text, inline link markup; `crawl4ai` has 220 rows
and **misses the mayor row entirely**. Counting rows rewards markup verbosity, not
whether the data survived.

### One thing this round cannot settle, by construction

trafilatura's non-wiki median (2 658 ch) against the challengers' 9–23k is not the
challengers doing well — it's stock trafilatura's known listing-page failure, the
thing our fallback ladder exists to fix. A round against **stock** trafilatura
flatters newcomers on half the corpus. Two modes run instead: `/challengers`
(trafilatura on one side, as asked) and `challengers-ours` (`ours` is, the
comparison the decision actually rests on). Both pin one entrant per pair, so three
newcomers cost **three** pairs, not the six a round robin would spend.

### Pre-registered, before voting starts

At ~12 votes/pair this round can answer "is this newcomer clearly worse than what we
ship" and cannot answer "is it better" (the wiki 3-way at 12 votes/pair couldn't
separate trafilatura from wikiextractor either). Claiming *better* needs ~40 votes
on one pair. Report en/non-en splits separately — trafilatura's edge over
wikiextractor was 100% of resamples in English, 49% in non-English.

## Challenger votes, and the non-wiki instrument (session 6)

14 human votes. They split cleanly:

| slice | champion W | challenger W | draw | exact binomial p |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **wiki** (pinned) | **7** | 0 | 0 | **0.008** |
| non-wiki (unpinned) | 2 | 2 | 3 | 0.69 |

Per challenger on wiki: `markitdown` 0-3, `crawl4ai` 0-2, `docling` 0-2. Pooled 7/7
(p = 0.008) answers the pre-registered question with **yes, on wiki, for all
three** — it does not rank the three against each other (2-3 votes each) nor claim
trafilatura is better by margin, only that it was not beaten once. Agrees with the
structural metrics, computed independently and before any vote.

### The non-wiki votes are excluded, and the reason is structural

The reviewer said they could only vote on wiki because "the rest is changing too
much" — a mechanical cause:

```
live wiki pages pinned to ?oldid=  : 50 / 50
live non-wiki pages pinned         :  0 / 50
non-wiki raw snapshots fetched     : 2026-08-18T08:32   (two days before labelling)
```

The reference pane is a live iframe: on wiki it shows the pinned revision (byte-for-
byte what the extractors read); on non-wiki it shows *today's* page while extractors
read a two-day-old snapshot, so those 7 votes judged extractions of a document the
reviewer could not see. A broken instrument, not noisy labelling — 2-2-3 is what
coin-flipping looks like. **Excluded.** Consequence: whether challengers beat stock
trafilatura on listing pages (2 658 vs their 9–23k) remains untested. Fix: serve the
saved snapshot in the reference pane instead of the live URL.

## Why not `scrapling-md` (session 6)

**What it is.** Scrapling's own `extract --md`: `Convertor._extract_content(selector,
"markdown", main_content_only=True)` — markdownify over `<body>` after noise-tag
stripping. Registered `layer="floor"`, labelled "no content model at all". Entered
as a **baseline**, not a candidate, so the rubric's over-extraction warning has a
referent.

**Its record is real, and entirely on the broken slice.**

| pool | non-wiki | wiki |
|---|---|---|
| human | 6W 1L 2D | **0W 2L 0D** |
| llm | 13W 2L 0D | *no votes* |

Every win is on the 50 unpinned pages established as unlabellable; on the 50 pinned
wiki pages it is 0-2.

**Structurally it is the over-extractor the rubric warns about.** Wiki medians:

| | trafilatura | ours | scrapling-md |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| chars | 79 629 | 79 628 | **247 604** |
| ATX headings | 32 | 32 | **19** |
| table rows | 32 | 32 | 64 |
| bare URLs | 0 | 0 | 28 |
| nav markers | 0 | 0 | 4 |
| image markers | 0 | 0 | 33 |

3.1× the text and *fewer* real headings — losing on both, not trading one for the
other. On `List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population` it opens with
`[Jump to content]`, `Main menu`, `move to sidebar`, `Navigation` and the entire
Wikipedia sidebar: **214 754** chars against trafilatura's **60 363**.

**A finding about the panel, not just the tool.** The LLM pool went **13-2** *for*
the over-extractor despite a rubric stating outright that reproducing the whole page
"trivially has perfect coverage and is still a poor extraction" — the panel read
length as coverage anyway. Independent evidence for the judge-validity gap the
self-consistency replicate is meant to size.

### Two corrections to earlier claims in this log's own narrative

1. **`scrapling-md` does not inherit the Scrapling fetch-layer defect as tested.**
   The entrant declares `variants=("raw",)` and reads the same saved `raw.html` as
   every other extractor, so the fused-citation defect measured at the fetch layer
   doesn't apply here. Fair caveat: shipping Scrapling's converter would probably
   mean shipping its fetcher too, and inheriting it then — a deployment argument, not
   a measurement.
2. **"7th in the human pool on 4 votes"** was the closed-set figure.
   `subset_standings` restricts to the 72 pages where all eleven entrants produced
   output, discarding 7 of `scrapling-md`'s 11 human votes. Full human record is
   **6W-3L-2D**. Closed-set is right for comparability, wrong for quoting a
   head-to-head record.

## How different are the tools, before anyone votes? (session 7)

The arena answers "which is better", never "is there anything to choose between them
at all" — where two tools return the same thing a vote is wasted. Built for two
deck slides, but became the cheapest real result in the study: no judges, no
sampling noise, no rubric.

`research/scrapers/similarity.py`, two measurements kept deliberately apart:
**word-token cosine** (lowercased word tokens, blind to markdown syntax/whitespace/
punctuation — *did these two select the same content*) and **byte-exact identity**
of the markdown (sensitive to everything). They disagree, and the disagreement is
the finding: cosine alone says the four fetchers are interchangeable (comfortable
and wrong); byte-identity alone says they're all different (true and useless).
Together: same words, different bytes, and the bytes are a bug in one of them.

### Fetch layer — a solid block, not an identity matrix

One converter (`trafilatura`, `favor_recall`) over each fetcher's saved body, 89
pages where all four returned markdown, 5 self-disagreeing dynamic pages excluded.

| | avg chars | cosine vs httpx | byte-exact vs httpx |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| httpx | 61 454 | — | — |
| Scrapy | 61 454 | 100.0% | **100.0%** |
| Scrapling | 61 432 | 100.0% | 53.9% |
| Scrapling · Stealthy | 61 538 | 98.2% | 45.1% |

All four within **0.2%** on volume — the expected near-identity matrix turned out
dark everywhere. Nobody finds text the others miss; the only surviving divergence is
the browser's DOM re-serialisation, costing 1.8%.

**Correction to an earlier number.** The "identical markdown: Scrapling 55.9%,
Stealthy 16.8%" figures came from `report.fetcher_agreement()`, hashing
`normalised_hash(html)` — *visible HTML text*, not markdown — out of the
`snapshots` table, a column only ever filled for 43–45 of 100 pages: a thin,
unstated slice at the wrong layer. `similarity.py` reads the `fetchcmp-*.html`
bodies off disk instead. Conclusion unchanged; the number was not trustworthy as
quoted.

### Extractor layer — two blocks, and they barely overlap

Identical HTML in, 100-page live corpus, own-coverage means:

| tool | pages | avg chars |
|---|---:|---:|
| goose3 | 83 | 13 024 |
| justext | 82 | 43 217 |
| ours | 100 | 55 033 |
| trafilatura | 97 | 56 641 |
| resiliparse | 96 | 60 278 |
| html-text | 100 | 69 629 |
| readability-lxml | 93 | 86 901 |
| crawl4ai | 97 | 111 846 |
| scrapling-md | 97 | 164 009 |
| markitdown | 97 | 171 543 |
| docling | 97 | 180 027 |

A **13× spread** from the same input. The cosine matrix splits into two blocks:
**content models** (`ours`, `trafilatura`, `resiliparse`, `justext`, `html-text`,
`goose3`) agree at 73–97%; **document converters** (`crawl4ai`, `scrapling-md`,
`markitdown`, `docling`) agree at 83–99%; across the divide they share only
**44–71%** of their words — not a ranking difference, a different document.
`readability-lxml` sits in the gap (54–57% to content models, 70–75% to converters)
— a partial over-extractor, consistent with its 86 901-char mean.

Three consequences: (1) **`markitdown` is 98.6% the same text as `scrapling-md`**
(byte-exact 0.0% — same words, different syntax); `scrapling-md` was an explicit
"keep everything" floor, so the challenger round's verdict on markitdown doesn't
rest on its 0-3 record — it *is*, to within 1.4% of its words, the floor planted on
purpose. (2) The whole challenger conclusion is reachable without votes: `docling`
at 90.5% and `crawl4ai` at 84% to that same floor are in the same family; the 7-0
wiki sweep is consistent with this, not load-bearing for it. (3) Had this run
first, the vote budget would have gone differently — six of eleven entrants agree
at 73–97% (hard to discriminate, worth votes); instead much of the budget went on
pairs 50% apart, which any judge separates for free.

`deck.html` rebuilt to the reviewer's own narrative (24 slides), with `build_deck.py`
inlining screenshots as data URIs. Two rendering defects surfaced only by
screenshotting the result (an inherited-opacity bug washing out bar-chart colours;
a tall screenshot breaking scroll-snap and pushing a footnote off-viewport) — both
fixed; a recurring lesson worth keeping is that source review missed both.

## The open comparison, and the presentation (session 8)

**The similarity matrix retargeted the vote budget.** The only comparison still
genuinely open is *inside* the content-model family, between the three tools
sitting 88–97% from each other: `ours`, `trafilatura`, `resiliparse`. A new
`content3` focus mode runs that as a 3-pair round robin, **wiki-only by
construction** (`sampler.focus_matchup` gained `wiki_only`) — the reference pane is
a live iframe and only the 50 wiki pages are pinned. `WIKI_ONLY = {"content3"}`
should shrink to empty once the pane serves the saved snapshot for non-wiki too.

| | count |
|---|---:|

| pages the unrestricted mode would have offered | 96 |
| existing votes inside the wiki population | 3 human, 0 panel |

## Is the fetch-layer agreement believable? (session 13)

Challenged directly — four scraper libraries returning ~100% the same words "sounds
like nonsense". The answer: real, mechanically expected, and **corpus-dependent in
a way that must be stated**.

**Why.** Three of the four are the same HTTP GET with the same user agent — httpx,
Scrapy's downloader and Scrapling's default fetcher send one request and read one
response body; the server can't tell them apart. They differ in scheduling,
retries, concurrency, cookies, parsing helpers — none touching the response body.
The surprising result would have been divergence. The only entrant that *can*
legitimately differ is the browser (executes JS, re-serialises the DOM) — the only
sub-100 figure in the matrix (98.2%).

**And this corpus does not need a browser.** Measured over 97 pages with both a raw
and rendered extraction, trafilatura output length ratio (rendered/raw): **median
1.000**; pages where rendering adds ≥25% text: **1** (`mnb.hu`, 405→851 chars);
pages where rendering *loses* ≥20%: **3** (`tagesschau.de` 7 475→684, `bpb.de`, MDN).

**Three scope limits, now on the slide:** (1) on a JavaScript-heavy corpus this
would not hold — SPAs/infinite-scroll would leave HTTP clients holding an empty
shell; untested here. (2) nothing bot-walled us hard — all four reached 98/100, the
two refusals defeated the browser too; a Cloudflare-heavy corpus would separate
Scrapling/Stealthy immediately. (3) "same" means same **words**, not same bytes —
only Scrapy is byte-identical to httpx; Scrapling is 53.9% and the difference is a
real defect (299 fused citation markers); word-cosine is deliberately blind to
that.

New deck slide, **"The scraper barely matters"**, placed as the Part 1 conclusion:
same words, same coverage, therefore an operations choice, with the mechanism/JS
caveat as footnote — the claim now bounded instead of implied. Running principle
kept for future decks: the audience reads the title and one word per element,
nothing else — anything needing a sentence belongs in the footnote or the
presenter's mouth.

## A race in the arena UI, found by screenshotting it (session 14)

Building a deck slide out of a live screenshot of `/content3` returned **a gov.uk
page** with the mode dropdown reading "content 3-way (wiki)" — a real defect.
`arena.js` runs `restoreMode(); loadMatchup();` at module scope; the `/content3` and
`/floorcheck` routes then inject a script that, on `DOMContentLoaded`, sets the
select's value and dispatches `change`, firing a **second** `loadMatchup()`. Neither
load was sequenced, so whichever response landed last painted the panes — in a
wiki-only mode that means being served an unpinned page whose reference iframe no
longer matches what the extractors read, exactly the failure `wiki_only` exists to
prevent.

Fixed with a monotonic `loadSeq`: each `loadMatchup()` takes a ticket and discards
its own response if a newer load has started. After the fix `/content3` served
`Baroque?oldid=1365570930` with the badge reading `content3 · ours vs trafilatura`.

**Did it corrupt any data? No.** Every one of the 45 `content3` votes and all 14
`floorcheck` votes are on `is_wiki=1` pages, so no out-of-mode vote was ever
recorded — luck, not design (the race only bites when the first request is slower,
and votes don't record their mode, so the report couldn't have detected it). Gap
worth noting: a `mode` column on `votes` would make this class of bug auditable
instead of invisible.

## Established results

### Fetch layer — measured twice, at two layers

**Layer 1: raw HTML.** Self-consistency controlled (same client fetched twice); 5
pages excluded as changing on every fetch.

| pair | content identical |
|---|---|
| httpx vs httpx (control) | 100.0% (95/95) |
| httpx vs Scrapy | 98.9% (92/93) |
| httpx vs Scrapling `Fetcher` | 75.3% (70/93) |
| httpx vs Scrapling `StealthyFetcher` | 16.8% (16/95) |

75.3% is not high enough to call the fetchers interchangeable, and an earlier
version of this log did exactly that. **Divergence is still not quality** — session
2 asked the question at the layer we actually ship.

**Layer 2: the markdown.** The settled post-audit trafilatura config
(`include_tables`, `include_formatting`, everything else off) run over each
fetcher's saved body, output compared byte-for-byte against the httpx body's output.
Script: `scratchpad/fetcher_x_extract.py`, bodies at
`.local/scraper-arena/snapshots/<page>/fetchcmp-<fetcher>.html`.

| fetcher | markdown == httpx (all) | (excl. 5 dynamic) |
|---|---|---|
| httpx repeat (control) | 98/100 | **95/95 = 100%** |
| Scrapy | 96/98 | **93/93 = 100%** |
| Scrapling `Fetcher` | 55/98 | 52/93 = **55.9%** |
| Scrapling `StealthyFetcher` | 47/100 | 44/95 = 46.3% |

**The divergence got worse, not better, at the markdown layer**: 23 pages differed
in normalised HTML, 43 differ in markdown — `fetch_layer.normalised_text` collapses
whitespace before hashing, blind to the dominant defect.

**What Scrapling actually costs.** Of the 43 differing pages, **38 are
byte-identical once all whitespace is stripped**. Scrapling returns an
lxml-reserialised DOM and drops whitespace-only text nodes between inline elements —
on Wikipedia, the space after every citation superscript (e.g. `notes.[79] Dorothy`
becomes `notes.[79]Dorothy`):

| | httpx / Scrapy | Scrapling (both fetchers) |
|---|---|---|
| fused citation boundaries (`[79]Word`) | 137 | **299** |
| pages where the count rose | 0 | **36** |
| pages where markdown differs at all | 0 | 43 of 98 |
| table rows lost | 0 | 54 (34 van Gogh, 19 Python, 1 Berlin) |
| table rows gained | 0 | 26 (Danube name table) |

**Concentrated on Wikipedia: 39 of 50 wiki pages differ (36 whitespace-only) versus
4 of 48 non-wiki.** Same class of defect as the `include_links` finding below (a
lost word boundary) but caused by the *fetcher*, invisible to any size check (one
character each). The two lost infoboxes are the only non-cosmetic damage; the
gained Danube table shows this is noise, not a consistent bias.

**Verdict: httpx or Scrapy. Not Scrapling as a plain fetcher.** Same success rate
(98/100), same latency (209ms vs 198ms median), buys nothing. Scrapy matches httpx
on content but not wall clock: 5,687ms median per request, 481s for 100 URLs, against
198ms / 26s — AutoThrottle and default politeness, tunable, but no reason to switch
either. `StealthyFetcher` is a different instrument, not a competitor: only 3 of its
53 divergences are whitespace-only, so its differences are real content — for pages
whose content genuinely isn't in the HTML.

### Speed

~50× spread: `resiliparse` ~3.6ms vs `ours` ~181ms per page. `ours` needs a browser
(~1.8s total/page vs trafilatura's ~0.9s) and ~52 CPU-hours/1M pages vs ~17.

### Judging system

Online Elo (live board) + Bradley-Terry MLE with bootstrap CIs and a symmetric
shrinkage prior. Two pools — `human` and `llm` — **never merged**, separate sampling
histories. Sampler weights pairs by `p(1-p)/√(1+n)` with a 10% uniform floor.
Byte-identical outputs auto-draw without consuming a vote. Automated panel = local
Claude Code Sonnet subagents (never API — hard requirement).

**Human vs panel agreement** — 117 human votes, 208 panel votes, overlapping on only
**37 comparisons**:

| measure | value | baseline |
|---|---:|---|
| exact (all 4 labels) | 64.9% | — |
| equivalent (draws collapsed) | 67.6% | chance 55.4% |
| **decisive only** (both named a winner) | **24/31 = 77.4%** | coin flip 50% |
| Cohen's κ, correct label space | **0.272** | 95% boot CI **−0.03 – 0.56** |

77.4% is real: one-sided binomial **p = 0.0017**, 95% CI 63–92%. Not guessing, but
not a substitute either — disagrees with us on about **one comparison in four**, and
rank correlation between the two boards is only **+0.53** (was reported as +0.55; it
drifts as votes accumulate — regenerate with `just arena-report`). Largest
disagreements: `scrapling-md` (human 1557 vs panel 1337) and `resiliparse@rendered`
(human 1388 vs panel 1644).

> **Correction: κ was previously reported as 0.638, "substantial agreement". That
> was wrong, and wrong in the flattering direction.** `report.py` labelled each
> verdict with the *winning entrant's name*, so chance agreement was computed as if
> `goose3` were an available answer on a page where goose3 was never shown.
> Spreading the marginals over 12+ entrant names drops expected agreement to ~11%
> and inflates κ. The choice set a rater actually faces has three options — left,
> right, neither. Relabelling by position in the alphabetically-sorted pair gives
> **κ = 0.272 on the same 37 cells**. Fixed in `report.py:judge_cell_agreement`; raw
> rates were never affected. κ is also deflated by skewed marginals here (25 of 37
> human verdicts went to the same slot) — the honest reading is "the panel beats a
> coin flip, and κ cannot distinguish it from moderate agreement either way at
> n=37".

**We never measured our own consistency.** Zero cells were judged twice by the same
judge in either pool — `by_cell` in `report.py` even dedups them, and there was
nothing to dedup. No estimate of the ceiling exists: 77.4% is clearly above chance
and of unknown quality relative to a human agreeing with themselves. A blind
re-judge of ~20 already-decided human cells is the cheapest missing measurement in
the whole study.

Position bias 52.5% left over 80 decided votes — negligible; order-swapped cells
agreed *more* (73.3% vs 63.6%), arguing against shared position bias inflating
agreement. Length-bias Spearman −0.52 (judges prefer *shorter* output, so wins
aren't a length artifact). Panel never used `both_bad` (0/195) vs human 2/17 —
unexplained asymmetry.

## Defects noticed along the way

Recorded for reproducibility, not as a result. None is a finding about the tools
under test — most are our own harness — and the flag audit is where the actual
answer lives.

**Session 1 (2026-08-18)**

1. **trafilatura global LRU dedup** — `deduplicate=True` drives a *process-global*
   LRU. The 4-calls-per-page timing protocol degraded output: 17,028 → 618 chars on
   the 4th call (96% loss), leaking *between* entrants. Fixed by resetting extractor
   state before every call.
2. **goose3 empties 36 → 17** — its `post_cleanup()` whitelist deletes all children
   of Parsoid `<section>`-wrapped Wikipedia HTML (Ada Lovelace 608k → 196 chars).
   Fixed via `known_context_patterns`, applied *only* as retry-on-empty (applying it
   always regressed 16 working pages).
3. Timing gate read aggregate CPU, so one pinned core at 6% passed a 25% threshold.
4. `.modal{display:flex}` beat `[hidden]{display:none}` — the closed overlay ate
   every click and silently blocked all mouse voting.
5. Bradley-Terry rated a 1-0 record at 2817 (unbounded MLE) — fixed with a prior.
6. Leaderboard defaulted to the empty `human` pool, showing flat 1500 while 208 LLM
   votes sat unused.
7. Mode selector reset on refresh — ~9 duel votes silently recorded as explore.

**Session 2 (2026-08-19)**

8. **`report.py` focus-vote counter** — tested `reason in ("focus","duel")`, but
   named focus sets tag as `wiki3`. Reported 0 of 36 wiki3 votes as focus-mode.
   Display-only; ratings unaffected.

**Session 3 (2026-08-19, later)** — both *measurement* defects in our own harness:

9. **Cohen's κ computed over the wrong label space** — see *Judging system*
   correction above; raw agreement rates were never affected.
10. **`fetch_layer.normalised_text` collapses whitespace before hashing** —
    deliberate, and right for its original purpose (it fixed a false 79-of-93
    divergence caused by regex tag-stripping) — but blind to the *dominant* real
    defect, Scrapling's whitespace-node drop. The headline "75.3% identical" was
    measuring the one thing that could not see the problem. Not a code fix — the
    comparison was re-run at the markdown layer instead.

**Session 4 (2026-08-19, later still)**

11. **`snapshot_text` newline translation** — `read_text(encoding="utf-8",
    errors="replace")` collapses `\r\n` to `\n`, so the string every extractor
    receives is not byte-identical to the file whose sha256 is recorded. 14 of 100
    pages affected, all CRLF-serving `*-other` sites, 0 wiki. No conclusion is
    affected (every entrant reads through the same function) but the stored sha
    can't verify what the extractors consumed, only what's on disk. Deliberately
    *not* fixed: `newline=""` would change every entrant's input and invalidate the
    161 surviving votes to fix a labelling nicety.
12. **wikiextractor-v2 returns nothing on Spanish Wikipedia** — España, Madrid,
    Sagrada Família all extract to 0 chars. Confirmed pre-existing, not caused by
    pinning. 3 of 50 wiki pages, all `es`, no other language affected.
    Uninvestigated; the non-English wiki comparison is quietly missing its three
    Spanish cells for that entrant.

## The judging rubric, rebuilt from the reviewer's labels (session 5)

The reviewer stated their hierarchy explicitly after ~53 explore-mode labels:
(1) **Data and tables** — did one method find the data and the other not (a missing
large table is bad); if both found the data but one preserved structure, prefer the
structured one; (2) **Markdown formatting**; (3) **Noise** (URLs, unwanted
characters). Crucially, **not a lexicographic tiebreak**: a markdown method that
missed one table on a very long document still earned a draw, because the markdown
helped so much across the whole thing.

Their own labels agree, though thin — over 37 decisive votes, the winner had more
markdown links in 81% of pairs where the two differed, more characters in 68%, more
bullets in 65%, more ATX headings in 63%. Pipe-table rows sat at 53%, nearly blind
here since plain-text entrants have no pipes (20 of 37 pairs tie at zero).

### What was changed

- `RUBRIC` rewritten: criterion 1 is data-and-tables (was it found, then was
  structure kept); criterion 2 markdown formatting; criterion 3 noise. The
  non-lexicographic trade-off stated outright, with the reviewer's own draw example
  in the prompt.
- `both_bad` offered in the brief — already valid in `VERDICTS` but the brief only
  ever showed `1 | 2 | draw`, why the v1 pool has exactly zero against the human's
  11%.
- Budgets 6k/8k → 8k/30k. At 10k a judge saw 5.7% of a 178k readability output, so a
  lost table almost always fell inside the elided window — criterion 1 wasn't
  actually judgeable. Runs as local Sonnet subagents, so this costs context, not
  money.
- **Structural inventory** added to every brief: whole-output counts of characters,
  headings, setext underlines, pipe-row runs and rows, list items, markdown links,
  bare URLs, citation markers/escapes, alongside the page's own HTML `<table>`/`<tr>`
  counts as ground truth — computed over the whole string, never the excerpt. The
  inventory carries a caveat: zero pipe-rows means no table *markup*, not
  necessarily missing data (a plain-text tool may keep every value as running
  text) — without saying so it would teach judges to punish plain-text entrants for
  the wrong reason.

### Pools are versioned, never blended

The 102 v1 votes moved to a `llm-rubric-v1` pool rather than deleted or merged:
`load_votes()` defaults to the human pool and the report reads `llm` explicitly, so
a third label is invisible to every existing code path. The 10 byte-identical
auto-draws stayed in `llm` (string equality doesn't depend on a rubric). Because
refreshed briefs keep their matchups, 97 cells now carry a verdict under both
rubrics.

### What the change actually did

| | human | rubric v1 | rubric v2 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| votes | 61 | 102 | 99 |
| first-position win rate | 50% | **63%** | 52% |
| draw or both-bad | 34% | 15% | 19% |
| both-bad | 11% | **0%** | 11% |
| Cohen's κ vs human | — | +0.346 | +0.417 |
| raw agreement vs human | — | 63.6% | 66.7% |

Position bias is the clearest win: v1 picked the first-shown output in 55 of 87
decisive verdicts (brief order is randomised, so this cost precision rather than
biasing any entrant); naming the measured number and telling judges to re-read
OUTPUT 2 took it to 52%. Be careful with the κ improvement (0.346 → 0.417): it rests
on only 22 and 21 shared cells respectively, far too wide an interval to call a real
gain — **raw agreement barely moved** (63.6% → 66.7%). The honest reading is the
pool's *marginals* now match the human's (draws and both-bad at roughly human
rates) and κ rewards that; whether it agrees on the actual verdict more often isn't
established at this sample size. Of the 97 cells judged under both rubrics, 75%
kept the same verdict; of the 24 that moved, 8 went winner→no-winner and 5 the other
way — consistent with legitimising `both_bad`, not reversing preferences.

### Two defects the repin left behind

Both the same shape: a page id is `uuid5` of its URL, so pinning the wiki set to
`?oldid=` permalinks minted new ids and *dropped* (not deleted) 50 old pages.

- `pending()` listed **149 briefs for dropped pages**; `refresh` skipped them (can't
  rebuild a brief for a page it can't load), so they'd sit forever as
  un-refreshable work, and a judge dispatched at one would record a vote against a
  page no longer in the corpus. `pending()` is now corpus-aware; `record()` refuses
  such a brief outright.
- `report.subset_standings` built its population from the run index alone. Dropped
  pages keep their extractor runs, so the language split raised `KeyError` on the
  first unresolvable id and `judge focus` couldn't run at all. Population is now
  intersected with the live page set.

### Standing of the v2 pool

Too thin to separate the field: 99 votes over 66 possible pairs is ~1.5 votes/pair.
In the 11-entrant closed set only 55 votes qualify; `scrapling-md` leads at 7–0–0,
its interval (1693–1934) barely clearing trafilatura's (1507–1702). Nothing below
the top is separated at all.

### Not measured, and not for want of asking

The reviewer asked whether Azure was available for a stronger judge. Not in this
environment: no `.env`, no `AZURE_AI_*`, and the Foundry chat client was
deliberately removed (`docs/architecture-decisions.md:206`) for cost quota, a
~125k tok/min rate limit, content filters on source text, and non-reproducibility
without credentials. Two of those bite this use case directly: at a measured mean of
44k input tokens per pair the rate limit caps throughput near 3 votes/minute, and a
content-filtered response is a *lost* vote correlated with page topic — worse than
random. Measured payload, for whenever the question returns:

| | input tokens | @ $1.25/Mtok | @ $3/Mtok |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 votes | 4.4M | $5.50 | $13 |
| 200 votes | 8.8M | $11 | $26 |

6% of pairs exceed 128k tokens on their own; 1% exceed 256k.

## The flag audit (session 2) — the main result

Session 1 diagnosed `favor_precision` and the glued-links defect but **did not
change the flags**. Session 2 measured each and removed three. Principle applied:
*keep only switches that add markdown structure; leave content selection to
trafilatura's own cascade.*

| flag | verdict | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `include_tables=True` | **keep** (already the default) | captures the Wikipedia infobox |
| `include_formatting=True` | **keep** | pure serialisation, no selection effect |
| `include_links=True` | **removed** | 6,230 fused links + 6,578 split sentences across 57 pages; 0 with it off |
| `favor_precision=True` | **removed** | disables trafilatura's own stage-3 baseline rescue *and* stage-4 recall escalation. 13 pages collapse below 500 chars, all listing/gov/institutional |
| `deduplicate=True` | **removed** | 0 cost in a single pass; latent process-global LRU hazard (bug 1) |
| `include_images=True` | **removed** | destroys table cells — see below |

**`favor_precision` collapses** (chars, before → after removal): tagesschau
7,475→45→**7,434** · BBC 7,327→168→**7,323** · RTV SLO 6,941→327→**6,941** · EU
countries 2,658→194→**6,088**.

**`include_images` destroys tables.** In trafilatura 2.0.0 an image inside a table
cell replaces the cell's text:

| | with images | without |
|---|---|---|
| empty `![]()` markers | 1,164 across 55 pages | **0** |
| table rows | 3,948 | **4,197** |
| rows with a usable first cell | 3,823 (96.8%) | **4,197 (100%)** |

249 rows vanish, 374 lose their identifying column. On *List of countries by
population* "India", "China", "United States" are deleted, leaving numbers attached
to nothing. The infobox does **not** depend on this flag — it comes from
`include_tables` and is identical either way.

### `_remove_common_junk` removed (2026-08-19)

The bs4 pre-clean was deleted outright rather than patched. Measured cost/benefit
before removal:

| | without | with |
|---|---|---|
| median chars | 13,809 | 13,809 — identical |
| pages collapsing <500ch | 12 | 12 — identical |
| **zero-padded table cells** | **8** | **112** |
| cookie/consent words | 57 | 43 |

Entire benefit: 14 cookie words corpus-wide. Cost: 104 corrupted table cells.
Cause: Wikipedia pads sort keys with `<span style="visibility:hidden;">0</span>`;
trafilatura drops them, but the BeautifulSoup round-trip re-serialises attributes in
a different order and they survive → `| 01 | Berlin |`. The `accessibility` junk
term separately deleted real headings (`## Accessibility` on MDN, `##
Accessibility features` on Comparison of web browsers). The two effects never
overlapped — padding hit German Wikipedia, cookie removal hit 3 gov/municipal pages
where the listing branch already replaces the banner. Trafilatura strips
script/style/noscript itself, so nothing else there was load-bearing.

After removal, padded cells in `ours` = 5, in stock trafilatura = 5. Identical.

`ours` is now **byte-identical to stock trafilatura on 89 of 100 pages** (was 22).
Median 14,014 — the same as stock, to the character.

100 — the source log does not reconcile this against the "89 of 100" figure above):

## Open — not yet done

### 0. A wiki-focused round against crawl4ai, markitdown and docling

Proposed design. Wikipedia is the right place: 50 of the 100 corpus pages are wiki,
it's where the two unresolved questions already live (non-English is a coin flip,
the Scrapling defect is wiki-specific), and infobox tables are the hardest thing any
of these tools has to do.

**Note:** session 6 (above) subsequently ran a challenger round covering crawl4ai,
markitdown and docling, including a wiki slice (7 human votes, p = 0.008) — but not
with the full design below (en/non-en stratification, dialect normalisation
verification, a self-consistency replicate, or the ~50-vote target). This item is
left as originally written, per this file's policy of not pruning the Open section;
treat the parts below not covered by session 6 as still open.

- **Feed them the saved HTML, not the URL.** `crawl4ai` is a fetcher *and* an
  extractor; if it fetches its own copy, the comparison confounds the two layers —
  the fetch-layer result shows that confound is worth 44% of pages, not a rounding
  error. Every entrant must read `raw.html` from the snapshot, same as the other 12.
  `crawl4ai` accepts `raw:<html>` and `file://` in `arun()`; `markitdown` takes a
  stream with an explicit `text/html` hint; `docling` takes an `InputDocument` from
  bytes.
- **Each needs its own interpreter.** `crawl4ai` pins Playwright and `docling` pulls
  model weights, fighting the project env — same problem `scrapling` and
  `wikiextractor-v2` already have. Use the existing precedent: `justfile`'s
  `arena-env` `--with` list for the light ones, out-of-process adapter for the heavy
  ones (`extractors/wikiextractor_adapter.py` is the pattern). `markitdown` is light
  enough for `--with`.
- **Align the markdown dialect before voting, or the arena scores markdown
  writers.** Already made this mistake once and fixed it: `MARKDOWNIFY_OPTIONS`
  exists because markdownify defaulted to setext headings and backslash escapes,
  producing 415 setext underlines and 384 stray escapes that humans were voting on.
  `crawl4ai` has its own markdown generator and `docling` its own serialiser, both
  with their own conventions. Normalise heading style and escaping first, and record
  what was normalised.
- **Challenger format, not a round robin.** 6 wiki entrants is 15 pairs; at 12
  votes/pair (the wiki 3-way's rate) that's 180 human votes. Instead run each
  newcomer against `trafilatura` only, with `wikiextractor-v2` as a second
  reference — 3–6 pairs, ~12 votes each, ~50 human votes. Pre-register what that
  buys: the wiki 3-way at 12 votes/pair couldn't separate trafilatura from
  wikiextractor (91.8%, not settled), so 12 votes answers "is this newcomer clearly
  worse than trafilatura" and **not** "is it better" (claiming better needs ~40
  votes on that one pair).
- **Stratify and report en/non-en split, pre-registered.** trafilatura's edge over
  wikiextractor is 100% of resamples in English, 49% in non-English — an aggregate
  over 25 en-wiki + 25 eu-wiki would hide exactly that.
- **Run the cheap structural metrics alongside the votes, not instead of them.** A/B
  missed both the padded-cell and the fused-boundary defects; scripted counts caught
  both in minutes. Per entrant per page: infobox captured y/n, table row count, rows
  with a usable first cell, fused-boundary count (`\[\d+\](?=\w)`), zero-padded
  cells, empty `![]()` markers, output chars. Objective, need no votes, and the
  reason the flag audit converged at all.
- **Also cheap and still missing: a human self-consistency replicate.** Re-serve
  ~20 already-decided human cells blind. Without it, the panel's 77.4% has no
  ceiling to be measured against.

### 1. `clean_markdown`'s cookie line-filter drops real content

`clean_markdown` deletes any *line* containing the word "cookie". After the
pre-clean removal this is the **only** non-listing difference left between `ours`
and stock — on *Comparison of web browsers* it removes: "Starting with version 4,
Google Chrome can disable Cookies, Images, JavaScript, Plugins, Popups, and
Geolocation individually." and "Most Gecko browsers have options to block chosen
images and cookies." Same class of bug as the `accessibility` junk term: a banner
heuristic firing on an ordinary content word. Lower stakes (2 hunks on 1 corpus
page) and not yet changed.

superseded. Fresh tally: trafilatura 12, ours 3, same 8, both_bad 1 — but read

### 3. Reference screenshots drifted 3.7h from the extracted HTML

`snapshot.py --screenshots-only` was re-run after the original pass. Screenshot is a

## Tried and rejected

- **Full fetcher × extractor cross-product as an *arena*** (3 × 8 = 276 pairs) —
  rejected at planning: near-all pairs auto-draw, diluting votes to ~7/pair at 2000
  votes. Still the right call. But rejecting the *voting* cross-product was
  silently read as rejecting the *comparison*, and that was wrong: running the same
  extractor over each fetcher's HTML and diffing the output costs no votes, takes
  about a minute, and is what finally surfaced the Scrapling defect. Cheap scripted
  comparisons and expensive human comparisons are separate budgets.
- **Scrapy vs `ours` head-to-head as a vote** — meaningless; different jobs.
  Comparing their *extracted output* under a fixed extractor is not, and is now
  done.
- **goose3 `known_context_patterns` applied unconditionally** — tried, reverted;
  regressed 16 working pages. Kept as retry-on-empty.
- **First "exact agreement" metric** — conflated display side with judgment,
  reporting 35%; corrected to 64.9%.
- **Original judging rubric** — ranked coverage first and called noise "minor",
  which would have let `html-text` (no boilerplate removal) win by default.
  Recalibrated before scaling up.
- **Whole-page A/B for config changes** — the two configs agree on most of a
  document, so the reviewer reads mostly identical text. Replaced by hunk review.

## Tooling built

**Session 1**: corpus sampler, snapshot pipeline, 12 extractor adapters, fetch-layer
benchmark, Elo/BT rating with bootstrap CIs, information-weighted sampler, blind
page arena, Sonnet judging panel, calibration + agreement reporting, `arena-duel` /
`arena-wiki3` / `arena-focus` closed-set modes.

**Session 2**: `arena/hunks.py` — line-aligned diff hunks with shared-run
collapsing, section breadcrumbs, and a unified inline view when both sides share
≥50% of words (24 tests). Hunk arena was made the default at `/` here; **session 4
swapped it back**. `hunk_votes` table (separate from page votes — hunks within a
page aren't independent, so the tally is counts, not an Elo), with output SHAs
recorded so stale votes are detectable. Reference pane can serve the exact snapshot
variant the extractor read. Judging cost per hunk pair dropped from reading two
~250k-char documents to ~27 localised decisions; 48% of hunk text was collapsed as
agreed before the image fix.

**Session 3**: `scratchpad/fetcher_x_extract.py` — fetcher × fixed-extractor
comparison over the saved `fetchcmp-*.html` bodies, with whitespace-only /
table-row / fused-boundary classification. Not yet promoted into
`research/scrapers/`; belongs next to `fetch_layer.py` as a second reporting stage.

**Session 4**: `arena/hunks.py:mark` — whole-document diff marking for the page
arena, with word-level refinement inside related lines and a byte-exact
reassembly guarantee (54 tests, `tests/test_scraper_arena_marks.py`); diff
navigation and collapse in the page arena UI. `snapshot.py --buckets` (restrict a
pass so a re-fetch cannot invalidate votes on pages it was not meant to touch) and
`--no-screenshots`; `snapshots.frameable` with a migration; `run_extractors.py
--no-timing` (one call per cell instead of warmup + 3 timed, ~4x faster — the pass
that produced this session's outputs had paid the full 2,400-extraction timing
cost for nothing).

**Session 6**: `extractors/challenger_worker.py` (out-of-process adapters under
`uv run --no-project --with <tool>`, JSON in/out, importing nothing from the arena)
and `run_challengers.py`; `sampler.focus_matchup(champion=...)` so N newcomers cost
N pairs instead of N(N+1)/2; `/challengers` arena route.

**Session 7**: `similarity.py` — per-tool char means and the two pairwise matrices
(word-token cosine, byte-exact) for both layers, reading fetcher bodies off disk
rather than the partially-filled `snapshots.sha256` column. `build_deck.py` —
inlines the presentation's screenshots as data URIs so the published page is
self-contained.
