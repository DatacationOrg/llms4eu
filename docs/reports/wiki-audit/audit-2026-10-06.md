| # | question | finding | so what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do gold pages over-represent famous places? (5) | Mildly, not SynthTRIPs-strong. Median sitelinks of gold pages equals the corpus median in every large language; question-weighted mean 3.1-3.2 vs 2.9 for all dev pages (excl. bot). The one under-sampled group is one-language Swedish pages: 65% of sv dev pages have a question vs ≥ 90% elsewhere | scores are not inflated by famous places; obscure Swedish lake stubs are thin |
| 2 | Are questions concentrated on few places? (5) | No: effective places / nominal = 0.86, top 10% of pages hold 20% of questions, HHI close to uniform. A per-page cap (≤ 5 easy + 5 hard) prevents concentration by design | dataset-side concentration is not an issue; concentration must be measured on *retrieval output* (needs a run) |
| 3 | Do documentation and notability agree? (6) | Weakly: Spearman sitelinks ~ char_count 0.22-0.36 in large non-sv languages (0.43 overall). In Swedish, `tourist_appeal` is unrelated to sitelinks (-0.02) and negative with char_count (-0.23): long bot pages are long, not interesting | keep `sitelinks` and `char_count` as separate breakdowns; never call either "popularity" |
| 4 | Is selection harder for obscure places? (9) | Overall yes: challenge 57% (1 sitelink) vs 90% (6-10), also without bot pages. **But within de, fr, it, es the gap is ≤ 5 points**; it is large only within fi (37% → 64%), pl (53% → 86%) and sv (64% → 81%), the lake-heavy languages | most of the overall gap is composition (language, page type), not popularity as such; report the breakdown within language |
| 5 | Is selection harder for short pages? (9) | Yes, excl. bot: challenge 65% (2nd char quintile) vs 89% (5th). Corpus questions do not depend on length (93-97%) | documentation volume affects selection, not availability |
| 6 | Availability vs selection (8, 9) | Availability is near-solved everywhere (corpus 89% fi to ~100%); selection ranges from 43% (fi) to 95% (nl) | compare methods on selection, per language; corpus questions only catch broken indexes |
| 7 | Can cross-border questions be tested? (7) | Barely: 5.1% of 745 dev geo questions have gold in more than one country, 6.3% in more than one language. Highest AT (18%) and SK (16%); 0% for SE, LT, HU | H1/H2 (geo vs language bias) need a hand-written or newly generated cross-border probe |

### What this dataset changes compared with the proxy corpus

Against "what the proxy data can and cannot support" in the literature consolidation (2026-10-06, §4):

| proxy could not support | this dataset |
|---|---|
| a real popularity contrast | **yes, partly**: sitelinks 1 to 136, but only 0.2% of pages have 31+ and the gap is mostly composition (finding 4) |
| recommendation-style questions | **no**: meta questions are exhaustive sets from metadata, without preferences |
| labels beyond one locality | **yes**: 133k pages, 26 languages, 27 countries |
| human judgments | **no**: all labels from LLMs; a 40-row human-check sample is drawn (Part 4) |
| real user language | **no**: generated questions; median `lex_overlap` 0.73 even for challenge questions |
| per-place metrics | **yes**: every page is one Wikidata id, so place = page |
| cross-border experiments | **thin**: 5% of geo questions cross a border (finding 7) |

## Part 6: The `realistic` label on hard questions

`realistic` is one of the 13 quality labels (Bunny or Ling, one call per page). The hard-question filter (`challenge_ok`) **ignores** it, and the labeller's overall `verdict` too, so unrealistic questions stayed in. Its definition in `qspec.SPEC` (tmp_labeling, not in the repo) is one line: *Would a real visitor or curious person ask the question in `item`, rather than a database or trivia question?*, judged with the full article in view, as one of 13 labels in one call per page. Easy questions are always `realistic = true`: any flag dropped them.
